## Why no referral is needed

Physiotherapy is a licensed profession in Thailand. Physiotherapists must be registered and licensed by the Physical Therapy Council under the Physical Therapy Profession Act B.E. 2547 (2004). Thailand allows direct access, which means you can see a physio without a doctor sending you.

World Physiotherapy's profile of Thailand (2024 data) sets this out. It reports that:

- the licence is granted by the Physical Therapy Council through a national examination
- people can refer themselves to physiotherapists in private practice
- people can also refer themselves in the public system

So a physio can assess and treat you without a doctor sending you. A good physio will also tell you when you need a doctor instead.

## When a referral still matters: insurance

Thailand doesn't require a referral, but your insurance policy might. Some international health plans only pay for "prescribed" physiotherapy. Allianz Care's Flexicare benefit guide (valid from 1 June 2026), for example, defines its prescribed physiotherapy benefit as treatment by a registered physiotherapist "following referral by a doctor". Physio is limited to 12 sessions per condition before your therapist or referring doctor reviews your treatment.

If your insurer wants a medical certificate, ask whether the clinic can issue one. [Pain Away Clinic](/bangkok/physiotherapy-clinics/pain-away-clinic/) in Bangkok, for example, lists an initial consultation with a medical certificate at ฿1,100, against ฿500 without.

Travel insurance varies even more. Before your first session, check:

- whether your policy covers outpatient physio at all
- whether it needs a doctor's referral or a prescription
- whether you need pre-authorisation, and how many sessions are covered
- whether the clinic can bill your insurer directly, or you pay and claim back

## When to see a doctor first anyway

No referral doesn't mean a doctor is never needed. See a doctor or go to a hospital before physio if:

- **a fracture is possible**: you heard a crack, the area changed shape, or you can't put weight on it (NHS)
- **you have numbness or tingling, or the skin has changed colour or feels cold** around the injury (NHS)
- **you have a high temperature** alongside the pain (NHS)
- **you've just had surgery**: bring your surgeon's notes and rehab protocol to your first session, so your physio follows it

## What to check before your first session

- **Your physio's licence.** Ask for their full name and Physical Therapy Council licence number.
- **Your insurer's rules.** Get any referral before you start. A referral after the fact may not count.
- **The first-visit cost.** Clinics often charge for the consultation separately, and hospitals add a doctor's fee.
- **Your records.** Bring scans, reports and operation notes. They save time and repeat tests.

## FAQ

### Can I see a physiotherapist in Thailand without seeing a doctor?

Yes. World Physiotherapy reports that people in Thailand can refer themselves to a physiotherapist, both in private practice and in the public system. Dedicated clinics book you in directly and start with their own assessment.

### Do Thai hospitals need a referral for physiotherapy?

Not as a rule, but a hospital may start you with its own rehabilitation doctor. At Bumrungrad, rehabilitative medicine specialists carry out the diagnosis and plan treatment. Ask the hospital how to book.

### Will my insurance pay for physio without a referral?

It depends on your policy. Some plans pay for self-referred physio. Others only cover physio a doctor has prescribed, often with a cap on sessions. Check your policy wording, or ask your insurer, before your first appointment.

### How do I know my physio is properly qualified?

Physiotherapists in Thailand must be licensed by the Physical Therapy Council, which grants licences by national examination (World Physiotherapy). Ask for your physio's name and licence number, and check with the Council if you're unsure.
