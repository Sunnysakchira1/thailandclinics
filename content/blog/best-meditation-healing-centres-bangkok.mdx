## The 5 centres, compared

### 1. Saksit Meditation and Healing Centre: best for a structured healing course

**Pranic Healing school · B.B.D. Building, 626 Rama IV Road, Bang Rak · 4.7★ on Google (6 reviews)**

[Saksit Meditation and Healing Centre](https://www.saksitphcentre.com/) teaches Pranic Healing, the no-touch, no-drug energy-based system developed into its modern form by Grandmaster Choa Kok Sui. Saksit's curriculum runs to 19 certificated courses across three tracks: Healing and Protection, Prosperity and Abundance, and Spirituality. Most courses last one or two days, from 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.

The entry course is Basic Pranic Healing Level 1. It runs for two days, needs no experience, and is open to anyone aged 16 or over. The fee is 9,500 Baht, and it includes the book, course materials, an internationally approved certificate, lunch, snacks and refreshments. From there the main path runs to Advanced Pranic Healing (Level 2), Pranic Psychotherapy (Level 3) and Pranic Crystal Healing (Level 4), and each level requires the one before. Saksit also holds Meditation on Twin Hearts sessions that need no course to attend, and individual Pranic Healing sessions for people who don't want to learn.

Saksit is the only centre on this list that publishes its whole curriculum, the prerequisites for each course and an all-in fee for the entry course. That makes it the easiest place to plan a course around a stay in Bangkok.

**Worth knowing:** Saksit doesn't name its teachers on its website, so ask who will teach your course. It has only six Google reviews. The site doesn't say which language classes are taught in, so check before you book. The fee for individual healing sessions and the Twin Hearts schedule aren't published. There's no online booking: you register by phone, WhatsApp or LINE on +66 92 272 7096. Pranic Healing doesn't replace medical care. Saksit says the same thing on its own site.

**Best for:** people who want to learn a healing practice step by step, with a certificate at each level.

### 2. Wat Mahathat Section 5: best for free daily meditation in the old town

**Vipassana centre inside Wat Mahathat Yuwaratrangsarit · Tha Phra Chan, Phra Nakhon**

Section 5 (Kana 5) at Wat Mahathat is a long-established place to learn vipassana, the insight meditation of the Thai Theravada tradition. It runs on donations, and visitors can join daily morning and afternoon practice with instruction for beginners. It's day practice only, with no overnight stays. The temple is a short walk from Tha Chang pier, near the Grand Palace.

The same temple also runs the International Meditation Practice Center, which takes online registration for residential programmes. Visitors describe a three-day, two-night programme for foreigners with food and accommodation provided.

**Worth knowing:** Wat Mahathat doesn't publish an English timetable on its official site, and the times quoted elsewhere don't agree. Call +66 2 222 6011 before you go. Cover your legs and shoulders. White clothing is preferred. Some visitors say English instruction is patchy at the residential centre.

**Best for:** first-timers who want to try vipassana at a temple without paying a fee.

### 3. Buddhadasa Indapanno Archives: best for a free monthly English mindfulness morning

**Buddhist archive and practice centre · Vachirabenjatas Park, Chatuchak · 4.8★ on Google (1,000+ reviews)**

The [Buddhadasa Indapanno Archives](https://main.bia.or.th/_en/taste-of-liberation/) are the Bangkok centre of the Suan Mokkh tradition, set in Vachirabenjatas Park, about 10 minutes' walk from MRT Phahon Yothin. Its English programme, Taste of Liberation, runs on the first Sunday of each month from 9:30 a.m. to noon. It covers mindfulness practice, a walk through the grounds and exhibits, and a food offering to the monks. Register by email at tasteofliberation.bia@gmail.com.

**Worth knowing:** the English session runs once a month. Most other activities are in Thai. The cost isn't published, and there's no visitor car parking.

**Best for:** a gentle introduction to Thai Buddhist mindfulness in a quiet green setting.

### 4. Bangkok Shambhala Meditation Center: best for a weekly drop-in after work

**Mindfulness-awareness meditation group · 5.0★ on Google (37 reviews)**

The [Bangkok Shambhala Meditation Center](https://bangkok.shambhala.info/) teaches shamatha-vipashyana, a mindfulness-awareness practice from the Tibetan Buddhist tradition, open to people of any faith or none. Its 2026 calendar shows a Wednesday evening session from 6:30 to 8 p.m. that combines meditation, a reading and discussion, in person or online. There's also a Sunday drop-in from 2 to 3 p.m. with instruction for first-timers. You don't need to register. The suggested donation is ฿150–300.

**Worth knowing:** the street address isn't on the pages we could check, so confirm the venue on the centre's website before you go. Sessions are short, which suits a habit but not a retreat.

**Best for:** expats who want a regular weekday practice and a community to sit with.

### 5. House of Dhamma: best for one-to-one vipassana in English

**Private meditation teaching · Lat Phrao Soi 15, Chatuchak · 5.0★ on Google (5 reviews)**

[House of Dhamma](https://www.houseofdhamma.com/) teaches vipassana in the Mahasi tradition, entirely in English, in a private house in Lat Phrao. Its prices are published for 2026:

- **One-day taster**, 10 a.m. to about 4:30 p.m.: ฿4,500 per person in a group of 2–5, or ฿5,000 on your own.
- **Two-day introductory course**: ฿8,000 per person in a group, or ฿9,000 on your own.
- **Usui Reiki** courses by appointment: from ฿15,000 for Level 1.

**Worth knowing:** there are no walk-ins. You book by email several days ahead, with a ฿1,000 deposit. There's no accommodation, and lunch isn't included on the taster day. It costs far more than the temples, and you're paying for private, English-language teaching.

**Best for:** people who want personal guidance in English rather than a group class.

## Meditation centre or healing course?

A **meditation centre** teaches you to train attention. Temples teach vipassana in the Thai Buddhist tradition, usually for a donation, while groups like Shambhala teach in a secular-friendly way. You can drop in, sit and leave.

A **healing course**, such as Pranic Healing or Reiki, teaches a set of techniques in levels, with a fee and a certificate for each one. It suits people who want to keep practising and build up skills over time.

Research on the health effects of each is very different. The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) says research suggests mindfulness meditation may help with stress, anxiety and sleep, though results vary between studies. There's little high-quality research on energy healing, so treat it as something you do alongside medical care, never instead of it.

## Questions to ask before you book

1. Which language is the session or course taught in?
2. Who is the teacher, and how long have they taught?
3. What does the fee or suggested donation cover?
4. Do I need to book, and how far ahead?
5. Is there a dress code, and are there rules for the day (silence, phones, precepts)?
6. For courses: what are the prerequisites for the next level, and do you offer a package?

## FAQ

### Where can I meditate for free in Bangkok?

Wat Mahathat Section 5, near the Grand Palace, holds daily vipassana practice for a donation. The Buddhadasa Indapanno Archives in Chatuchak hold an English mindfulness morning on the first Sunday of each month. The Bangkok Shambhala Meditation Center asks for a suggested donation of ฿150–300.

### Where can I learn Pranic Healing in Bangkok?

Saksit Meditation and Healing Centre in Bang Rak teaches Grandmaster Choa Kok Sui's full Pranic Healing curriculum, starting with Basic Pranic Healing Level 1 (two days, 9,500 Baht, no experience needed). Pranic Healing Thailand on Sukhumvit Soi 12 also teaches it.

### Is there English meditation in Bangkok?

Yes. House of Dhamma teaches every session in English. The Bangkok Shambhala Meditation Center holds English sessions every Wednesday evening and Sunday afternoon. Wat Mahathat Section 5 offers daily sessions for foreign visitors, and the Buddhadasa Indapanno Archives hold an English session monthly.

### What should I wear to meditate at a Bangkok temple?

Cover your shoulders and knees, and wear something loose enough to sit cross-legged in. White or light clothing is preferred at many temples but usually isn't required for a day visit. Take your shoes off before you enter the meditation hall.

### Can meditation or energy healing replace medical treatment?

No. The NCCIH says mindfulness meditation may help with stress, anxiety and sleep, but it doesn't replace treatment. There's little high-quality evidence for energy healing. Use either alongside the care your doctor recommends.
